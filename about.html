From reporting to research

I began reporting in 2009 while studying mass communication in Kwara State. As an undergraduate at Bayero University Kano, I studied how Nigerian newspapers covered the state of emergency in the northeast during the Boko Haram insurgency. That project led to my 2014 essay Fourth Estate of the Realm or Wreck? in Peoples Daily. Working as a reporter gave me a practical sense of the choices that determine which stories reach an audience.

For my master’s thesis, I turned to hate speech on Twitter in Nigeria from 2015 to 2019. I compiled terms and expressions in Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, and Nigerian Pidgin. The work sharpened a question that still drives my research. How do people and the systems they use recognise harmful speech when its meaning depends on who is speaking, who is listening, and what they know about the context?

Current research

My dissertation examines how Nigerian users make sense of human and automated moderation and the role of state authorities in regulating online speech. I study how people respond when they believe a platform has unfairly restricted their speech, as well as how some users take advantage of gaps in enforcement to gain visibility or advance political interests.

In related work, I compare patterns of online hate speech with records of offline violence. I also study how community organisations use strategic communication to support peacebuilding, including the work of the Future Prowess Islamic Foundation in northeastern Nigeria.

Research practice

I build and code social media datasets and use content analysis to identify patterns across posts. I also work with interviews and focus groups to understand how people interpret events and why they act as they do. I want this work to inform academic research and industry practice, especially decisions about how platforms evaluate moderation across languages and give users a way to contest enforcement.

Community service and mentorship

During my 2015–2016 National Youth Service Corps placement in Jigawa, I proposed a media-literacy project through the Editorial Community Development Service group and helped organise outreach in Dutse. I also anchored NYSC Forum, a weekly Radio Jigawa programme where I discussed digital literacy. The NYSC Jigawa State Secretariat and Jigawa Broadcasting Corporation recognised this work with commendations.

Undergraduate projects supervised at Skyline University Nigeria

Academic and professional service

I have reviewed conference submissions for the National Communication Association and served as a reviewer for its African American Communication and Culture Division’s 2024 Outstanding Research Awards. My journal reviewing includes the International Journal of Research and Scientific Innovation and the International Journal of Research and Innovation in Applied Science.

I am an Associate Member of the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations and a member of the National Communication Association and the Association of Internet Researchers.

Leadership

Founding Head of Mass Communication, 2021–2023

At Skyline University Nigeria, I helped develop the curriculum, led the department’s work on programme accreditation, and helped establish radio and television studios. I oversaw the Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) and organised visits to Abubakar Rimi Television, Freedom Radio, Express FM, and other stations in Kano State.

Student publishing and broadcast production

I helped establish The Skyline Explorer and served as its general editor, mentoring student reporters. I launched Skyline Reflection, a television news magazine that gave students experience in broadcast production. I joined the editorial team of Newsline, the university’s official publication, in 2021 and served as editor-in-chief from 2022 to 2023.
