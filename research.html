Research

Advancing democracy in the Global South

I study the sociotechnical dimensions of digital technology, focusing on how digital platforms and AI shape democratic participation in the Global South. I’m interested in who gets heard in public debate, whose views are taken to represent the public, and how people can challenge decisions that limit their participation. Nigeria is the starting point for this work. Over time, I want to build comparative projects with researchers, journalists, and civil society organizations across the Global South.

Current research

My dissertation examines how social media creators in Nigeria understand and respond to human and automated content moderation. I ask how judgments about fairness shape their responses to enforcement and how incentives for visibility and income shape what they post. This work grows out of my research on political hate speech and peacebuilding communication in Nigeria. Together, these projects ask what it takes for people to participate in public life when speaking up carries unequal risks.

Platform governance and AI moderation

I examine how Nigerian users understand and respond to moderation, including how their tactics can keep them visible or exploit gaps in enforcement.

Digital politics and conflict

I study political hate speech in Nigeria and how patterns of online hostility compare with offline violence.

Strategic communication

I examine how organizations communicate across differences, including how a community organization in northeastern Nigeria uses communication to support peacebuilding.

Future research agenda

Whose views count as public opinion?

I plan to study how political views are selected and represented as they move from social media feeds into AI-generated summaries. Starting with public debates in Nigeria, I want to examine which perspectives gain visibility, which survive summarization, and whether those summaries make a divided public sound more unified than it is. I would draw on content analysis and other methods suited to each question, including interviews or surveys where they help explain how people interpret those debates. As the work develops, I may incorporate computational methods to trace patterns across platforms and languages at a larger scale. The aim is to help newsrooms, researchers, and AI teams assess whether summaries convey disagreement and reflect voices that receive less attention online.

What keeps democratic participation open?

I want to examine how moderation, reporting, and appeals affect people’s ability to participate in political debate across languages and platforms. I would study when enforcement protects people from abuse, when it restricts legitimate expression, and what users can recover after a successful appeal. The aim is to give civil society groups, policymakers, and platform teams evidence they can use to design better protections and remedies.
